### What their homepage does that yours doesn't

- **3,392 crawlable words** vs your 419.
- **Interactive age quiz above the fold** (ages 4–17). It captures the lead *and* creates a
  personalisation signal. The CTA is "Get a learning plan", not "Sign up" — much lower friction.

  Premium $20→$17/lesson, with discount percentages shown.

  plus a Trustpilot link and the claim "Over 940,000 parents worldwide trust Novakid".

  an indexable page targeting its own keyword cluster.

### Note on their pricing

Novakid's cheapest is **$9/lesson**, Premium up to **$20**. **You charge $8.** You are
cheaper than Novakid's cheapest tier and you are not advertising that anywhere a crawler
can see it.

## 3. Cambly Kids — winning on borrowed authority

Cambly Kids is the interesting case: only **899 words** and **zero hreflang tags** —
technically weaker than Novakid and Allright, yet still ranks.

**Why:** it sits on `cambly.com`, a domain with years of accumulated authority and backlinks
from the adult Cambly product. The `/kids` page inherits that domain strength.

You cannot copy this. It is the one advantage that has to be earned over time.

What they do execute well:
- **CEFR framework explained on-page** (A1→C1) — a recognised credibility standard.
- **Named tutors with visible ratings** (Ana 4.92, Renee 4.88, Russell 4.93, Trevor 4.90).
- **kidSAFE Seal certification** — a third-party child-safety badge. Strong trust signal for
  parents, and one you could realistically obtain.
- **A specific, verifiable success story** (Raaid — 10,000 minutes, won a Silicon Valley app
  competition). Far more persuasive than generic praise.

## 6. What to do, in priority order

### Priority 1 — Expand homepage content to 1,500+ words
The single highest-impact fix. Add to the pre-rendered HTML: how lessons work, teacher
credentials, curriculum detail, what makes you different, expanded FAQ. Right now you are
10x thinner than Allright.

### Priority 2 — Age-tier landing pages
`/english-for-ages-4-7`, `/8-11`, `/12-16`. All three competitors segment by age; a parent
searching "English lessons for 7 year old" needs a page that says exactly that.

### Priority 3 — Publish pricing where crawlers can read it
Novakid shows 8 price tiers on the homepage. You are cheaper and hiding it. Build
`/pricing.html` as static HTML and make the $8 comparison explicit.

### Priority 4 — Add hreflang
You serve Philippines, China and are targeting Poland with 0 hreflang tags. Novakid has 47.
This directly affects which country sees which version.

### Priority 5 — Named testimonials with photos and countries
All three do this. Collect 5–6 from real parents, publish with first name + country.

### Priority 6 — Consider city pages for Poland
Allright has 286 Polish city pages. You could build a focused set for the top 10 Polish
cities rather than 286 — enough to compete locally without thin-content risk.

⚠️ Do this carefully: mass-generated near-identical pages can be treated as doorway pages
and penalised. Each page needs genuinely distinct, useful content.

### Priority 7 — Apply for kidSAFE certification
Cambly displays this seal. For a children's platform it is a strong, verifiable trust signal
and directly answers the "is this site safe?" concern parents and AI assistants raise.

## 7. Realistic assessment

Novakid and Allright have **years of content and backlinks**. You will not outrank them for
`online english for kids` any time soon, and chasing that head term wastes effort.

Where you *can* win within months:

- **Long-tail:** "one to one online english tutor for 8 year old", "cambridge english tutor
  online for kids", "online english class philippines"
- **Comparison:** "novakid alternative", "novakid vs", "cheaper than novakid" — you have a
  genuine price advantage and an existing comparison page to expand
- **Price-led:** "$8 english class for kids", "affordable online english for children"

The gap is content volume and trust signals — not product quality. Your platform is
competitive. Your website simply does not say so where Google can read it.
