# How to get real parent reviews

Copy-paste messages and a simple process. Nothing here involves writing reviews yourself.

---

## First: you may already have some

Your platform **already collects** star ratings and written comments. Parents rate a lesson
from their dashboard after it is marked completed, and that data has been saved all along —
there was just no way to see it in one place.

**Go to Admin → Parent reviews.** You will see your real average rating, every written
comment, and a list of parents who rated 4★ or higher.

If that page is empty, it means either no lessons have been marked **completed**, or parents
have not been prompted to rate. Both are fixable below.

---

## The process that actually works

Most parents are happy to review — they simply never think to. The three things that matter:

1. **Ask at the right moment** — right after a lesson that clearly went well.
2. **Ask the right people** — parents who already rated 4★ or 5★ in your dashboard.
3. **Make it one tap** — send the direct link, never "search for us on Trustpilot".

Realistic expectation: about **1 in 4** parents you personally ask will follow through.
So to get 10 reviews, ask around 40 families. Ask in small batches, not all at once.

---

## Step 1 — Turn lesson ratings on inside the platform

This is the easiest source because parents are already logged in.

**Message after a good lesson (Messenger or WhatsApp):**

```
Hi [Parent name],

[Child name] did really well in today's lesson — [specific thing, e.g. "she read a
full paragraph out loud without stopping"].

If you have a moment, could you rate the class in your TutorPro dashboard? Just open
the lesson and tap "Rate class". It takes about 10 seconds and helps us match [Child
name] with the right teacher and pace.

Thank you!
```

The specific detail matters. "She read a full paragraph out loud" gets a response;
"please rate us" does not.

---

## Step 2 — Invite your happiest parents to Trustpilot

Once a parent has rated 4★ or 5★ in your dashboard, invite them to review publicly.
The **Parent reviews** panel has a "Copy review request" button that fills this in for you.

**Trustpilot review link:**
```
https://www.trustpilot.com/evaluate/tutorpro.site
```

**Message:**

```
Hi [Parent name],

Thank you for rating [Child name]'s lesson [4/5] out of 5 — that genuinely means a lot
to our teachers.

We're a small school, and honest reviews from real parents are how other families find
us. If you have a minute, would you consider leaving a short review here?

https://www.trustpilot.com/evaluate/tutorpro.site

Anything you write is fine — even one or two sentences. Thank you either way!
```

**Email subject lines that work:**
- `Would you share your experience with TutorPro?`
- `A quick favour — 1 minute?`
- `Thank you for rating [Child name]'s lesson`

---

## Step 3 — Google Business Profile

Separate from Trustpilot, and this one puts you in Google's knowledge panel and Maps.

1. Create the profile at <https://business.google.com>
2. Once verified, Google gives you a short review link (looks like `g.page/r/...`)
3. Send that link the same way as above

Google reviews carry the most weight with parents searching on Google. Worth doing both.

---

## Step 4 — Ask for permission before publishing on the site

A Trustpilot review is already public. A comment left privately in your dashboard is **not** —
you need consent before putting it on your website.

**Permission message:**

```
Hi [Parent name],

Thank you again for your lovely feedback about [Child name]'s lessons.

Would you be happy for us to share your words on our website? We would show it as:

   "[their exact quote]"
   — [First name], [Country]

We would never publish your full name, email, or [Child name]'s full name. Just say yes
or no — either is completely fine.
```

---

## Rules — do not break these

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Never write reviews yourself** | Fake reviews breach Trustpilot and Google policy. Trustpilot removes them and can flag the business; Google can issue a manual action. |
| **Never offer discounts for positive reviews** | Incentivising *positive* reviews specifically is against Trustpilot policy. You may invite everyone to review honestly. |
| **Never publish without consent** | A private dashboard comment is not a public testimonial. |
| **Never edit the meaning** | Fixing a typo is fine. Rewriting is not. |
| **Do not filter out negative reviews** | Inviting only happy parents is fine. Deleting or hiding criticism is not. |
| **First name + country only** | Never publish full names, emails, or children's full names. |

---

## What to do with a bad review

You will get one eventually. It is not a disaster — a profile with only 5★ reviews looks
fake to parents.

1. **Reply publicly, calmly, within 48 hours.**
2. Acknowledge the specific issue, do not argue.
3. Say what you are changing.
4. Offer to sort it out privately.

A well-handled 2★ review with a thoughtful reply often converts better than a wall of
perfect scores, because it proves the reviews are real.
