## ⚠️ The one legal point that matters

Korean tutoring law restricts **who may teach inside Korea**, based on visa status:

- Foreigners **living in Korea** on an E-2 visa may not give private lessons.
- F-series visa holders may, if registered with the education office and paying tax.

**This does not apply to you.** Your teachers are in the Philippines, teaching from the
Philippines, and you are a Philippine-registered business (DTI 5274092). You are an
overseas provider serving Korean families online — the same position as Cambly, Ringle,
Preply and every other international platform operating openly in Korea.

**What you must do:** be transparent that lessons are delivered from the Philippines by
Philippine-based teachers. Do not imply your teachers are in Korea or that you are a
Korean 학원 (hagwon). Honesty here keeps you clearly outside the domestic tutoring rules.

## Where Korean parents actually look

Korea does **not** run on Google. Ignoring this is the single biggest mistake foreign
companies make.

### 1. 네이버 Naver — the search engine that matters

Naver holds the majority of Korean search. Google is a distant second for Korean-language
queries about local services.

Critically, Naver does **not** rank the open web the way Google does. It prioritises **its
own properties**, in this order:

1. **네이버 카페 (Naver Cafe)** — community forums
2. **네이버 블로그 (Naver Blog)** — Naver's own blogging platform
3. **네이버 지식iN** — Q&A, Korea's Quora
4. Everything else, including your website

**Implication:** SEO on tutorpro.site will do almost nothing for Korean traffic. You need
presence *inside* Naver. A Naver Blog is free and will outrank your own website for Korean
queries.

### 2. 네이버 카페 — where parents genuinely decide ⭐

This is the highest-value channel in Korea and the least understood by outsiders.

Korean mothers organise into enormous regional parenting cafés — 맘카페 ("mom cafés").
Some have hundreds of thousands of members. Recommendations inside these cafés drive real
enrolment decisions.

Notes from teachers who have done this successfully: getting your name into a relevant
Naver Cafe produces more students than any paid advertising.

**How to do it properly:**
- Join, then participate genuinely for weeks before mentioning your service
- Most cafés **ban outright advertising** and will remove you
- Answer questions about English learning, share useful tips
- Let parents ask you privately — that is how it converts
- If you have any Korean-speaking contact, have them do this. It does not work in English.

### 3. 카카오톡 KakaoTalk — the conversion channel

Effectively universal in Korea. Parents expect to message on KakaoTalk, not email.

- **KakaoTalk Channel** (카카오톡 채널) is the business equivalent of a WeChat Official Account
- Free to create, and Korean parents will actually use it
- This should be your primary contact method for Korean families, ahead of email

### 4. 인스타그램 Instagram — strong in Korea

Unlike China, Instagram is popular and unrestricted. Korean mothers in their 30s use it
heavily. Good for lesson clips, student progress, teacher introductions.

### 5. 유튜브 YouTube — fully available

No firewall issues. Korean parents research on YouTube extensively. Long-tail educational
content in Korean works well.

### The timezone advantage is significant

**Korea is UTC+9, Manila is UTC+8 — a one-hour difference.**

A Korean child's after-school slot of 17:00–21:00 is **16:00–20:00 Manila time**. That is a
completely normal working day for your teachers.

Compare with Poland, where the same after-school window means teaching at 23:00–03:00 Manila.

**Korea is the easiest market you could possibly staff.**

## Pricing: you are very competitively placed

Korean private English tutoring commonly runs **₩40,000–70,000 per hour** (roughly
$30–52 USD). Even established online platforms charge well above budget rates.

Your **$8 per 25-minute class** is approximately **₩11,000** — dramatically below the
domestic rate.

⚠️ Two cautions:
- Korean parents can associate very low prices with low quality. Lead with **Cambridge and
  Oxford curriculum, teacher vetting and one-to-one attention**, not with being cheapest.
- Consider showing prices in **won (₩)** for Korean visitors, the same way you added PLN.

## The prerequisite, stated plainly

**You need a Korean speaker.** Not machine translation.

Naver Cafés, KakaoTalk and Korean parents all operate in Korean, and Korean parents are
discerning about language quality. Awkward translated Korean reads as untrustworthy and
will actively hurt you.

Options: hire a Korean-speaking part-time marketer, partner with a Korean parent already
using your service, or find a Korean-speaking Filipino teacher on your team.
