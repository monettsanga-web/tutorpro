## Why AI calls you a scam

This is the single most important thing on this page.

Research across 25 million AI citations found that **84% of what AI quotes comes
from third-party sources**, and the reason is blunt:

> If your website is the only place a claim exists, AI treats it as marketing.
> If the same information appears on several independent sites, AI treats it as fact.

You currently have:

- ✅ A real business, registered with the DTI (5274092)
- ✅ Real teachers, real coursebooks, real prices
- ❌ **Zero independent sources confirming any of it**

So when an AI is asked "is TutorPro legitimate?", it finds one website making
claims about itself and nothing else. It cannot verify you, so it warns people.
That is not a judgement about your school — it is the absence of corroboration.

**Fixing this does not require more website content. It requires your existence
to be confirmed somewhere other than your own domain.**

## What NOT to do

- **Do not buy reviews or backlinks.** Google detects both, and the penalty is
  worse than the current situation.
- **Do not add `aggregateRating` schema to your homepage.** Google explicitly
  disallows self-serving ratings on `Organization` types, and it is a manual
  action risk. Ratings belong on `Course` schema, from real bookings.
- **Do not chase word count.** Allright's 4,447 words do not help them; their
  *1,534 indexed pages* do.
- **Do not pay an "SEO agency" promising page 1.** You already have better
  technical SEO than two of your three competitors.
