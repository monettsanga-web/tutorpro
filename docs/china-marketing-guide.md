# Promoting TutorPro to Chinese families

Researched August 2026. **Read the legal section first — it changes the whole strategy.**

---

## ⚠️ Read this before spending anything

In July 2021 China introduced the **"Double Reduction" policy (双减政策)**. It bans
**for-profit tutoring in core academic subjects — including English — for students in
grades 1–9** (roughly ages 6–15).

It also specifically prohibits **foreign-based teachers delivering paid lessons to Chinese
minors online**. This is what destroyed VIPKid's China business and cost 51Talk most of its
domestic market.

### What this means for you specifically

Your three age pages target **4–7, 8–11 and 12–16**. The middle two, and most of the third,
sit squarely inside the restricted band.

| Your segment | China status |
|---|---|
| Ages 4–7 (pre-school, under grade 1) | Grey area — less restricted, but "enrichment" framing matters |
| Ages 8–11 | 🔴 Restricted (grades ~2–5) |
| Ages 12–16 | 🔴 Mostly restricted (grades ~6–9) |
| Adults / university students | ✅ Fully legal |
| Overseas Chinese families (US, Canada, Singapore, Australia) | ✅ Fully legal |

### The honest risk assessment

Demand did not disappear — it moved into private WeChat groups. Many foreign tutors still
teach Chinese children, and enforcement targets **platforms and advertising**, not usually
individual foreign tutors.

But be clear-eyed:
- **Openly advertising English classes for Chinese children is the part that gets shut down.**
  Accounts get deleted, sometimes without warning.
- Parents are cautious because *they* can be penalised too.
- Payments can be frozen. Building your main growth channel on this is fragile.

**I am not telling you not to serve Chinese families.** I am telling you not to build a
public advertising campaign around under-15s in mainland China, because that is the exact
activity the policy targets.

## The three strategies that are actually safe

### ✅ Strategy 1 — Target overseas Chinese families *(strongest option)*

Chinese-speaking families in the **US, Canada, Singapore, Malaysia, Australia, UK and Hong
Kong** are:

- Completely outside Double Reduction
- Often wealthier and less price-sensitive
- Actively looking for English support for their children
- Reachable on the *same* Chinese platforms — Xiaohongshu and WeChat are used heavily by
  the diaspora

**This is the single best China-adjacent opportunity available to you.** Same language, same
platforms, same marketing materials, none of the legal exposure. Your `/cn/` page already
serves them.

### ✅ Strategy 2 — Adults and professionals in mainland China

Adult English learning is **fully legal and unrestricted**. Business English, interview
preparation, IELTS/TOEFL, conversation practice.

This would need a new offering — your platform is built for children — but the market is
large and there is no regulatory risk.

### ✅ Strategy 3 — Word of mouth and private referral inside China

Existing Chinese families introducing friends privately, through WeChat, is how this market
actually operates now. Your referral programme already supports it, and the QR codes now
work in China (fixed today).

This is low-risk because it is private, not advertised.

## The platforms, and how each is used

### 1. 小红书 Xiaohongshu / RED — best discovery channel

300M+ users, ~70% female, ~70% under 35 — overwhelmingly the mothers who make tutoring
decisions. Functions as a search engine for parenting and education decisions.

**How it works:** post genuinely useful content, not adverts. The algorithm punishes
promotional posts and rewards authentic value.

Content that performs for a school like yours:
- "3 ways to help a shy child speak English" (三个方法帮助孩子开口说英语)
- Short clips of pronunciation practice
- Explaining the Cambridge/Oxford curriculum difference
- Day-in-the-life of an online lesson

**Conversion path:** Xiaohongshu for discovery → WeChat for conversation → enrolment. Almost
never a direct sale on Xiaohongshu itself.

⚠️ Frame content as **enrichment, conversation and cultural exchange** — not "K-9 English
tutoring". The wording genuinely matters for account survival.

### 2. 微信 WeChat — where conversion happens

Not really a discovery channel; it is the relationship channel. Chinese parents expect to
message before buying anything.

- **Official Account (公众号)** — a business account for posting articles. Requires business
  verification; a foreign entity can register but it is slower and needs an agent.
- **Personal/service account** — how most independent tutors actually operate.
- **WeChat groups** — where the private tutoring market genuinely lives.

Your site already has **WeChat Pay QR support** and RMB pricing (RMB25/25min + RMB5 fee).

### 3. 抖音 Douyin — huge reach, high risk

The biggest audience, but the most aggressively moderated for education content. Former
teachers built million-follower channels here by pivoting to *general English content*
rather than selling lessons to children.

Only worth it if you can commit to consistent video, and keep content educational rather
than promotional.

### 4. Bilibili (B站) — good for longer content

Younger, tolerant of long-form educational video. Lower commercial pressure. Good for
"how to learn English" content that builds authority slowly.

### 5. 知乎 Zhihu — Q&A authority building

China's Quora. Answering questions like "如何提高孩子的英语口语?" (how to improve a
child's spoken English) builds long-term credibility and is indexed by Baidu.

---

## Baidu — the search side

Baidu is China's Google, ~60%+ market share.

**Blunt reality:** Baidu SEO is largely futile without an **ICP licence** and China-based
hosting. Baidu heavily deprioritises foreign-hosted sites, and Baidu's ad platform requires
a Chinese business licence.

Skip Baidu until you have an ICP filing. Xiaohongshu and WeChat are where the audience
actually is anyway.

---

## What I would actually do

**In order, and honestly:**

1. **Reframe toward overseas Chinese families.** Same platforms, same language, zero legal
   risk, better payment reliability. This is the real opportunity.
2. **Start a Xiaohongshu account.** Free. Post twice weekly in Chinese. Educational content
   only — no "sign up now" posts.
3. **Set up WeChat properly** as the conversion channel, and make sure your existing WeChat
   Pay flow works end to end.
4. **Lean on referrals** from any current Chinese families. Private, legal, and your QR
   codes now work in China.
5. **Do not run paid advertising** for children's English inside mainland China.

## What you would need that you do not have yet

| Requirement | Status |
|---|---|
| Chinese-language content for Xiaohongshu | Need to create — the `/cn/` page is a start |
| A Chinese-speaking person to post and reply | **Essential.** Machine-translated content is obvious and performs badly |
| WeChat Official Account verification | Needs a business entity or an agent |
| ICP licence (for Baidu SEO / mainland hosting) | Needs a Chinese entity; 2–6 weeks |
| Alipay / WeChat Pay merchant account | Needs Chinese business registration |

**The Chinese-speaking person is the real prerequisite.** Chinese social platforms are
relationship-driven and reward native fluency. Posting translated English content produces
almost nothing.
