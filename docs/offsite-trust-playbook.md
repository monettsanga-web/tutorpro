# Off-site trust playbook

Everything on tutorpro.site is now done. What remains is **off-site presence** — the signals
that substitute for domain age. This is the work that changes how Google and AI assistants
describe you.

Work through these in order. Total time: about 90 minutes, spread over a week.

---

## Task 1 — Google Business Profile (30 min) ⭐ highest impact

This creates an entity **Google itself verifies**. It is the single strongest answer to
"unverified independent operator", because the verification is Google's, not yours.

It also unlocks: knowledge panel on branded searches, star ratings in results, and a review
platform parents already trust.

### Setup

1. Go to <https://business.google.com> and sign in.
2. Click **Add business** → **Add single business**.
3. Enter these exact details:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Business name | `TutorPro Online English` |
| Business category | **Language school** (primary) |
| Additional categories | *Tutoring service*, *Educational institution* |
| Website | `https://www.tutorpro.site` |
| Phone | `+63 962 528 4849` |

4. **Critical — choose the service-area option.** When asked *"Do you want to add a location
   customers can visit?"* answer **No**. You are online-only. Adding a fake storefront address
   gets profiles suspended.
5. **Service area:** add *Philippines* plus the countries you teach (e.g. China, Poland,
   Hong Kong, Malaysia). You can list up to 20.
6. **Verification:** for service-area businesses Google usually verifies by **phone call or
   SMS**, sometimes video. Have your DTI registration to hand — video verification may ask
   to see proof the business is real. **DTI Reg. No. 5274092.**

## Task 2 — Ask your four parents for public reviews (20 min)

These four already recommended you publicly on Facebook. They have written the words once,
so the ask is small and the success rate is high.

| Parent | Reviewed | What they praised |
|---|---|---|

| Sharmila Maniam | Aug 2021 | 6-year-old, reading, writing, teaching method |

### Why this matters more than more Facebook reviews

Facebook recommendations are invisible to Google — I tested it: a logged-out fetch returns a
login wall with zero review content. Trustpilot and Google reviews are **publicly crawlable**
and are what parents check when comparing schools.

### Message — send individually, not as a broadcast

**To James King:**
```
Hi James,

Thank you again for recommending us back in 2021 — we still really appreciate it, and
we've just added your words to our website.

We're trying to help more parents find us, and honest reviews are the main way that
happens. Would you mind leaving the same review on Google or Trustpilot?

Google: [paste your g.page review link]
Trustpilot: https://www.trustpilot.com/evaluate/tutorpro.site

Either one is a huge help — even one or two sentences. Thank you!
```

**To Syafiqah / Snoopy / Sharmila** — same message, swap the name and the year.

### Tips that raise the response rate

- **Send one link, not two.** Pick Google for most people — it is the one parents search.
  Use Trustpilot for anyone without a Google account.
- **Send on a weekday evening or Sunday morning.** Weekday mornings get ignored.
- **Follow up once, after 5–7 days.** Once only. Never twice.
- Expect roughly **1 in 4** to convert. Four asks likely gives you one review, so keep asking
  every happy parent from here on.

⚠️ **Never offer a discount for a positive review.** Incentivising positive reviews
specifically breaches both Google and Trustpilot policy. Inviting everyone to review
honestly is fine.

## Task 3 — Ask your current parents too (10 min)

Do not stop at the 2021 four. **One review from 2026 is worth more than five from 2021**,
because it proves you have current customers.

Open **Admin → Parent reviews** in your dashboard. Any parent who rated a lesson 4★ or
higher is a proven advocate, and there is a one-click **"Copy review request"** button that
personalises the message with their child's name.

Ask after a lesson that clearly went well, and mention something specific:

```
Hi [Name],

[Child] did really well today — [specific thing, e.g. "she read a whole paragraph out
loud without stopping"].

If you have a minute, would you leave us a quick review? It genuinely helps other
parents find us:

[your Google review link]

Thank you either way!
```

Specificity is what gets replies. "Please review us" does not work.
