## Why it is written this way

- **The DTI number is stated openly.** Anyone can check it. Most tutoring
  sites do not do this, and it is your single strongest trust signal.
- **Real prices, no "contact us for pricing".** Parents comparing schools
  reward this.
- **The coursebooks are named.** "Cambridge" and "Oxford" mean something to a
  parent; "our proprietary curriculum" does not.
- **No claims I cannot back up.** No student counts, no success percentages,
  no awards. If Trustpilot or a parent checks any sentence here, it holds up.

> ⚠️ **Do not add star ratings or review counts to your own description.**
> Trustpilot removes them, and Google can penalise a site for self-declared
> ratings.

# PART 2 — Inviting parents to review you

## The rules you must follow

Trustpilot enforces these. Breaking them can get your reviews deleted or your
profile flagged.

| ✅ Allowed | ❌ Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Inviting every parent, the same way | Only inviting the happy ones |
| "Please share your experience" | "Please leave us 5 stars" |
| A gentle reminder | Offering a discount or free class for a review |
| Writing your own honest review of a service | Writing reviews as your parents |

**The biggest risk is cherry-picking.** If you only invite the parents you
know are delighted, that is called review gating and it is against
Trustpilot's guidelines. Invite everyone.

---

## Step 1 — Find your review link

1. Sign in at https://business.trustpilot.com
2. Your link is:

```
https://www.trustpilot.com/evaluate/tutorpro.site
```

Test it in your browser first so you know what parents will see.

---

## Step 2 — Decide who to ask

Start with parents whose child has **finished at least 3 lessons**. They have
enough experience to write something real.

Do not send to everyone at once. **Send 5 to 10 per day.** A sudden burst of
reviews on a brand-new profile looks manufactured and Trustpilot may hold
them for checking.

## Step 4 — One reminder, then stop

If there is no reply after **five days**, send one short reminder:

```
Hi [Name], just a gentle nudge about the review — completely fine if you
would rather not. Thank you again for trusting us with [Child's name].
```

**Never send a third message.** It damages the relationship, and one annoyed
parent costs you more than one review gains.

---

## Step 5 — Reply to every review

Reply to all of them, good and bad. Parents reading later notice this more
than the reviews themselves.

**For a positive review:**

```
Thank you so much for taking the time to write this. It means a great deal to
a small school like ours. We will pass this on to [Teacher's name].
```

**For a critical review — this one matters most:**

```
Thank you for telling us this honestly. You are right that [specific thing]
was not good enough, and I am sorry. Here is what we are changing: [specific
change]. Please contact me directly at sejongenglish@yahoo.com so I can put
this right for [Child's name].
```

Never argue. Never blame the parent. A well-handled complaint convinces more
readers than a five-star review — it shows what you do when something goes
wrong.

---

## What to realistically expect

- About **1 in 5** parents you ask will actually write a review.
- To get 10 reviews, plan on asking around 50 parents.
- **The first 5 are the hardest.** After that the profile looks established
  and people are more willing.
- Aim for **10 reviews in your first month**, then a steady trickle.

A profile with 8 honest reviews including one 3-star beats a profile with 40
that all read the same. Parents are good at spotting fake ones.

## Two honest warnings

1. **Do not buy reviews.** Trustpilot detects it, and a "Fake reviews
   detected" banner on your profile is far worse than having none.
2. **Do not write reviews yourself or ask family to.** Trustpilot matches IP
   addresses and device fingerprints. It gets caught.

The slow, honest route is the only one that survives.
