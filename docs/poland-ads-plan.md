## ⚠️ Read this first: the scheduling problem

Manila is **UTC+8**. Poland is **UTC+1 in winter (CET)** and **UTC+2 in summer (CEST)**.
That is a **7-hour gap in winter, 6 hours in summer**.

Polish children are available for lessons roughly **16:00–20:00 Warsaw time** (after school).
Here is what your teachers must be awake for:

| Polish child's slot (Warsaw) | Teacher must teach at (Manila) | Season |
|---|---|---|
| 16:00 | **23:00** same night | Winter |
| 17:00 | **00:00** midnight | Winter |
| 18:00 | **01:00** next morning | Winter |
| 19:00 | **02:00** next morning | Winter |
| 20:00 | **03:00** next morning | Winter |
| 16:00 | **22:00** same night | Summer |
| 18:00 | **00:00** midnight | Summer |
| 20:00 | **02:00** next morning | Summer |

**Weekend mornings are the easy win.** A Saturday 10:00 Warsaw lesson is only 17:00 Manila —
a completely normal teaching hour.

### What to do before spending money
1. Confirm **how many teachers will genuinely work 23:00–03:00 Manila**. If the answer is
   "one or two", cap your daily ad budget accordingly — you cannot fulfil demand you can't staff.
2. Consider a **night-shift premium** for those teachers, or recruit teachers in a
   Europe-friendly timezone.
3. **Push weekend slots hard** in the ads (Sat/Sun mornings Polish time = comfortable Manila
   afternoon/evening). This is your highest-margin, easiest-to-staff inventory.

If you cannot staff weekday evenings, **advertise weekends only** at first. Running ads for
slots you can't fill produces refunds and bad reviews.

## Market context

- Poland has a large, well-established private English tutoring market; parents are used to
  paying for after-school English ("angielski dla dzieci").
- Main competitors: local język schools (Early Stage, Helen Doron, Berlitz), plus online
  players (Novakid is very heavily advertised in Poland, Preply, italki).
- **Novakid is your closest comparison** — same model (online 1-to-1 English for kids, native
  and non-native tutors). Polish parents will compare you to them directly, so your pricing
  and trial offer need to make sense against theirs.
- Your **$8–$10 per 25 min** is competitive for the Polish market. At ~4.0 PLN/USD that is
  roughly **32–40 zł per lesson**, which sits below typical local in-person rates.

## Recommended budget

Start small and prove the funnel before scaling.

| Phase | Duration | Daily budget | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Test | Weeks 1–2 | **60–100 zł/day** (~$15–25) | Find which keywords/audiences convert |
| 2. Optimise | Weeks 3–4 | 100–160 zł/day | Kill losers, double down on winners |
| 3. Scale | Month 2+ | Scale only while cost-per-trial stays profitable | Growth |

**Split:** ~60% Google Search, ~40% Facebook/Instagram to start.
Google captures parents already searching; Facebook creates demand and is cheaper per click.

**Target metrics to watch**
- Cost per free-trial booking: aim **under 80–120 zł**
- Trial → paid conversion: aim **30%+** (this is where your teachers matter most)
- If cost-per-trial is fine but trial→paid is poor, the problem is lesson quality or
  scheduling, not the ads.

## Facebook / Instagram

### Targeting
- **Location:** Poland. Start with Warszawa, Kraków, Wrocław, Poznań, Gdańsk, Łódź
  (higher income, more online-education adoption), then broaden.
- **Age:** 28–48
- **Gender:** slight skew female — mothers usually make tutoring decisions, but don't exclude men
- **Detailed targeting:** Parents (with children 3–12), interests in *edukacja dzieci*,
  *Novakid*, *Helen Doron*, *nauka języków*, *Duolingo*, private schools
- **Placements:** Facebook Feed, Instagram Feed, Instagram Stories, Reels

### Primary text (Polish)
```
Twoje dziecko uczy się angielskiego, ale wciąż boi się mówić?

Na naszych lekcjach 1 na 1 dziecko mówi przez całą lekcję — nie siedzi cicho w grupie 15 osób.

✅ Indywidualny lektor dopasowany do dziecka
✅ Program zgodny z Cambridge i Oxford
✅ Raport dla rodzica po każdej lekcji
✅ Elastyczne terminy, również w weekendy
✅ Już od 32 zł za lekcję

Pierwsza lekcja próbna — bezpłatnie.
```

**Headline:** `Angielski 1 na 1 dla Dzieci`
**Description:** `Bezpłatna lekcja próbna. Zarezerwuj termin online.`
**CTA button:** `Zarezerwuj` (Book Now)

### Creative that works for this market
1. **Video (best performer):** 15–30s screen recording of an actual lesson — child on one
   side, teacher on the other, colourful courseware on the board. Add Polish subtitles.
   Most Polish parents have never seen inside an online kids' lesson; showing it removes doubt.
2. **Before/after testimonial:** a parent describing their child's confidence improving.
   Once you have Polish students, film these — they convert far better than stock imagery.
3. **Static:** happy child at a laptop with a headset + big text `PIERWSZA LEKCJA GRATIS`.

Avoid generic stock photos of Asian classrooms — Polish parents respond to imagery that
looks like their own home.

## Landing page requirements

Send Polish traffic to a **dedicated page**, not your generic homepage.

Your site now auto-detects Polish visitors by IP and translates the interface, and the
booking calendar now shows **Warsaw time automatically**. Still do these:

- [ ] Headline in Polish above the fold
- [ ] Show price in **zł** (your site now shows `≈ 32 zł` next to the USD price for PL visitors)
- [ ] **Show available times in Warsaw time** — done automatically now, but verify with a VPN
- [ ] Single clear CTA: `Zarezerwuj bezpłatną lekcję próbną`
- [ ] Trust signals: teacher qualifications, number of lessons taught, parent reviews
- [ ] Mention Cambridge/Oxford alignment — Polish parents value recognised standards
- [ ] Make sure PayPal works for Polish cards; **consider adding Przelewy24 or BLIK**,
      which dominate Polish online payments. Many Polish parents don't use PayPal.

**Biggest risk:** payment friction. BLIK is used by a huge share of Polish online shoppers.
If checkout is PayPal-only you will lose sales you already paid to acquire.

## Launch checklist

**Before you spend a single złoty**
- [ ] Confirm teacher coverage for Polish hours (see the timezone table above)
- [ ] Test the site end-to-end with a Polish VPN: does it show Polish, Warsaw time and zł?
- [ ] Book a test trial lesson yourself as a Polish visitor
- [ ] Install Google Ads conversion tracking + Meta Pixel, and fire a conversion event on
      trial booking — without this you are flying blind
- [ ] Decide the trial offer: free, or paid-but-cheap (a small fee filters out no-shows)

**Week 1**
- [ ] Launch 1 Google Search campaign, 3 ad variants, high-intent keywords only
- [ ] Launch 1 Facebook campaign, 2 creatives
- [ ] Add negative keywords daily for the first week
- [ ] Check search terms report daily and prune waste

**Week 2–4**
- [ ] Pause anything with high spend and zero conversions
- [ ] Scale the winners
- [ ] Start collecting Polish parent testimonials for creative

## Realistic expectations

- Expect the **first 2 weeks to lose money.** That is data-collection spend, not failure.
- Polish parents research carefully and often take **1–3 weeks between first click and
  booking.** Don't judge a campaign after 3 days.
- Your **trial lesson quality decides everything.** Ads only buy the trial; the teacher
  converts it into a paying customer.
- Start with a **small budget and weekend-heavy scheduling** until you prove teachers can
  reliably cover Polish hours.
